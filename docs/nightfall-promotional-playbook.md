# Nightfall Promotional Playbook

> **Working campaign platform:** *Your future does not need to begin with a list.*

Nightfall should not advertise a database, a chatbot, or a promise of admission. It should advertise the moment of relief when a student stops carrying an uncertain future alone. The product is a private companion that helps a student find a direction, understand a small research set, organize the work, and remain in control of every meaningful decision.

This document is a **creative and production direction**, not a media-buy recommendation and not an admissions claim. The suggested copy is deliberately grounded in product behavior: source-linked research, a guided Consultant conversation, student-owned shortlists, reminders, and approval-first university communication.

## 1. The campaign idea

### The emotional tension

The student does not wake up wanting “more university options.” They wake up with browser tabs, family expectations, language requirements, grades, deadlines, and an uncomfortable feeling that one wrong decision could cost them time. Most education marketing makes this anxiety louder by shouting rankings, acceptance claims, or endless lists.

Nightfall takes the opposite position:

> **The world is large. Your next step can still be clear.**

The brand should feel like a calm, unusually thoughtful person who sits beside the student late at night, narrows the noise, and says: *“Let’s start with what is true about you.”*

| Keep | Avoid |
|---|---|
| A private conversation | A lead-generation questionnaire |
| “A direction that feels like yours” | “We will get you admitted” |
| A small, source-linked research set | “10,000 universities at your fingertips” |
| Quiet confidence and editorial restraint | Urgency, hype, generic AI claims |
| Student ownership and clear approval | Autonomous-agent theatre |

### Primary line

**Your future does not need to begin with a list.**

Supporting lines rotate by context:

| Moment | English | Shami Arabic |
|---|---|---|
| Relief | Start with a real conversation. | بلّش بحديث حقيقي. |
| Direction | Find a direction before you collect options. | لاقي اتجاه قبل ما تجمع خيارات. |
| Trust | Research you can actually trace. | بحث بتقدر ترجع لمصدره. |
| Agency | You choose every next step. | إنت بتختار كل خطوة جاية. |
| Conversion | Meet the Consultant when you are ready. | قابل المستشار لما تكون جاهز. |

The Arabic should remain spoken, warm, and Levantine—not mechanically translated formal Arabic. “نايتفول ما بتقرر عنك” is stronger than an institutional phrase about “user autonomy.”

## 2. Campaign architecture

The campaign should move a cold viewer through four feelings, not four product features.

| Stage | Viewer thought | Creative answer | Product proof | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition | “This is exactly how application research feels.” | Tab overload, late-night notes, uncertainty | Calm Consultant opening | Watch / save |
| Relief | “Maybe I do not have to solve this alone.” | One quiet question, then space | Typed conversation and source links | Meet the Consultant |
| Trust | “This is not pretending it can decide my future.” | Student reviews reasons and sources | Explainable match signals and approval-first actions | Start a private conversation |
| Action | “I can take one useful step now.” | A shortlist becomes a next action | Saved programmes, calendar, document and Gmail controls | Join the early list / begin |

The visual device can be consistent across every format: **a wide, quiet field of near-black; one considered line of copy; moon-silver data or map detail; a decisive human next action.** The globe belongs only where it communicates possibility, never as filler.

## 3. Three launch creative routes

### Route A — The list is not the beginning

This is the launch route and should carry the largest share of early creative. It is a direct reframe of how students begin.

**Six-second vertical video.** Begin on a phone filled with open university tabs. Let the tabs disappear one by one into graphite. Cut to the Nightfall question: “What would you genuinely like to study?” Hold a beat. End on the line: “Your future does not need to begin with a list.”

**Voiceover, English:** “Before the shortlist. Before the deadlines. Before everyone tells you what to do. Start with a real conversation.”

**Voiceover, Shami Arabic:** “قبل القايمة. قبل المواعيد. وقبل ما الكل يقلّك شو تعمل. بلّش بحديث حقيقي.”

**End frame:** `NIGHTFALL / MEET THE CONSULTANT`.

### Route B — Someone has quietly narrowed the world down

This route makes the product tangible without turning it into a dashboard tour. It shows the transition from ambiguity to a restrained three-option research set.

**Carousel sequence.**

1. “You do not need fifty tabs.”
2. “You need a direction that feels like yours.”
3. “Three places to start. Every source still visible.”
4. “You decide what stays.”

The final slide should show an actual but non-personalized product state labelled **Illustrative research set**. Do not use university logos, fabricated testimonials, or claims that a student was accepted.

### Route C — Still yours

This route is Nightfall’s trust differentiator. It is strongest for parents, cautious students, and warm visitors who have already seen the product once.

**Fifteen-second vertical film.** Show a message draft, a calendar reminder, a document preview, and an official programme source. Each receives a simple statement:

> *Nightfall can prepare the work.*  
> *You approve every email.*  
> *You inspect every source.*  
> *You choose every next step.*

End with: **“Your future, held with care.”**

## 4. Social system

### Reels and TikTok

Treat short-form as a set of small cinematic observations, not a feature checklist. The editing should be patient: 0.5–1.0 seconds of tension, a clean reveal, then a single invitation. Use real interface recordings only when the privacy state is obvious and no personal data is visible.

| Format | Hook | Middle | Close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tab overload | “I had 37 tabs open for my future.” | The screen quiets into one question | “Start with the question.” |
| Parent pressure | “Everyone has an opinion about where you should go.” | Student chooses a study direction | “It still has to feel like yours.” |
| Source trust | “A recommendation is useless if you cannot check it.” | Official source opens beside a programme | “Keep the source close.” |
| Approval-first | “AI can draft. It cannot send for you.” | Draft → review → student decision | “Still yours.” |
| Night ritual | “The research always happens too late at night.” | Moonlit Earth, quiet Consultant screen | “A calmer place to begin.” |

Avoid trends that depend on sarcasm, fake urgency, or a loud reaction shot. The brand can participate in a native vertical language while still feeling composed.

### Instagram carousel

Carousels should read like editorial pages a student wants to save. One sentence per slide is enough. Recommended series:

- **Things no one tells you before you make a university list.**
- **A better question than “What universities should I apply to?”**
- **What does “a good fit” actually mean?**
- **Three ways to research a programme without trusting a random ranking.**
- **The things Nightfall will not decide for you.**

### Stories

Stories can be more conversational and interactive. Use polls as research prompts, never as a substitute for advice.

| Story prompt | Poll choices | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| “What feels hardest right now?” | Choosing a field / Too many options | “That is where a real conversation starts.” |
| “Would you rather…” | Three good options / Fifty random ones | “Exactly.” |
| “Should an AI send a university email for you?” | Never / Only after I approve | “Nightfall agrees.” |

## 5. Paid-social ad set concepts

Paid executions should make one promise per asset. Keep targeting and audience configuration outside this playbook; no campaign should imply individual academic eligibility, visa outcomes, funding, or admission likelihood.

| Asset | Primary copy | Visual | Landing destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1: cold student | “Your future does not need to begin with a list.” | Moving Earth and one question | Public landing hero |
| A2: field uncertainty | “Not sure what to study? Start there.” | Typed Consultant first turn | Consultant entry |
| A3: research anxiety | “Three source-linked places to start.” | Illustrative research cards | Consultant entry |
| A4: parent trust | “Nothing moves without the student.” | Approval-first email/source steps | Public landing trust section |
| A5: warm retargeting | “Ready to make the first step useful?” | Quiet saved option and calendar state | Consultant entry |

### Ad copy bank

**English primary text 1**

You are not behind because you have not found the perfect university yet. Start with what is true about you. Nightfall helps you turn that into source-linked places to explore—without pretending to decide your future for you.

**English primary text 2**

The first useful step is not another ranking. It is a real conversation about where you are, what you want, and what needs to work in your life.

**Shami Arabic primary text 1**

**Shami Arabic primary text 2**

أول خطوة مفيدة مش ترتيب جديد. هي حديث حقيقي عنك، عن اللي بدك تدرسه، وعن شو لازم يمشي بحياتك.

## 6. Event and campus material

Event material should behave like a doorway into a private moment—not a sales booth. Use one large line, a QR code, and a short human invitation. The visual hierarchy must be legible at three metres.

### Poster A

> **Your future does not need to begin with a list.**

> Meet the Consultant. Take the first step privately.

`QR / NIGHTFALL`

### Poster B

> **Too many options is not the same as direction.**

> Start with a real conversation.

`QR / START HERE`

### Table card

Front: `What would you genuinely like to study?`

Back: `Nightfall keeps the sources visible. You keep the decisions.`

Staff should never improvise outcome claims. The human script is simple: “It is a private first conversation. It helps students clarify a direction and open a small research set they can inspect themselves.”

## 7. Production rules

The first wave should use product recordings, typography, motion, sound design, and simple physical scenes—not stock students celebrating an acceptance letter. A student’s future is serious; the creative should respect that without becoming solemn.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Palette | Graphite, off-white, moon-silver only. No blue-globe or neon-AI visual clichés. |
| Type | Large editorial headline; small mono label; room around every idea. |
| Motion | Slow Earth turn, typed answer, source opening, a card settling into place. Never fast UI collage. |
| Sound | Subtle room tone, pencil, keyboard, soft transit, or low musical pulse. Silent viewing must still work. |
| Proof | Use illustrative states or consented internal demos. Never show personal student documents or claims of results. |
| CTA | “Meet the Consultant,” “Start a private conversation,” or “Save your place.” Avoid “Apply now.” |

## 8. Measurement and safeguards

Measure whether the marketing creates a qualified, trusting beginning—not simply the cheapest click.

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Landing → Consultant-start rate | Shows whether the promise creates enough safety to begin. |
| First-turn → final-local-interview rate | Shows whether the conversation feels worthy, not lead-gen. |
| Account-unlock completion rate | Reveals whether the value appears before the sign-in gate. |
| Source-open and save actions after unlock | Signals research intent, rather than empty curiosity. |
| Save/share rate for organic carousels | Indicates resonance with the student’s actual anxiety. |
| Qualitative replies | Captures language students use to describe their own uncertainty. |

Do **not** optimize toward promises of acceptance, “high chance,” or inferred personal attributes. Do not use uploaded academic records for advertising audiences. Do not retarget a student using sensitive interview details. If a campaign needs personalization, confine it to high-level, consented on-site context after the student has begun a private conversation.

## 9. First eight assets to make

1. A 10–12 second launch film: **Your future does not need to begin with a list.**
2. A 15-second typed-Consultant film: **Start with the question.**
3. A source-trust carousel: **Three things to check before you trust a programme recommendation.**
4. A student-agency film: **Still yours.**
5. An Arabic-first field-uncertainty reel.
6. An English/Arabic event poster pair.
7. A parent-facing approval-first story sequence.
8. A warm-retargeting static asset: **Ready to make the first step useful?**

The first production should launch only after the team confirms each asset has one job, one claim, one clear CTA, and no claim Nightfall cannot substantiate.
